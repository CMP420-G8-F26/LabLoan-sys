# Stakeholders

## Overview

This document identifies the people and groups who are affected by or have an interest in the Laboratory Equipment Loan and Return System.

---

## S1 — Students

**Role:**
Primary users of the system and the main borrowers of equipment.

**Interest / Expectation:**
Ease of navigation and use, accurate information about their loans and equipment availability, and reminders about their return dates.

**Influence on the System:**
Low. Students do not make decisions about the final system, but their feedback can be helpful in improving it.

**How the System Affects the Stakeholder:**
The system removes the need for students to track their borrowed items manually or visit the lab to check availability. It also reduces the risk of forgetting to return items, as overdue equipment is flagged automatically.

---

## S2 — Laboratory Technicians and Staff

**Role:**
Main operators of the system. They review loan requests, process checkouts and returns, and monitor the equipment inventory.

**Interest / Expectation:**
To reduce paperwork and manual tracking of items. They want a clear and organized view of pending requests, currently borrowed items, and overdue equipment.

**Influence on the System:**
High. If the system does not fit their workflow, the borrowing process will break down. Their requirements directly shape how the checkout, return, and request management features are designed.

**How the System Affects the Stakeholder:**
The system reduces the amount of work for staff by replacing manual logs with automated digital records, while also reducing the risk of human error.

---

## S3 — Laboratory Administrators

**Role:**
Administrators oversee the entire process and equipment inventory. They are responsible for managing user accounts and equipment records.

**Interest / Expectation:**
A clear and accurate view of the inventory state at all times. They also want access to detailed history reports of borrowing activity, including student information, equipment condition, loan duration, and more. Managing user permissions and roles within the system is also a key expectation.

**Influence on the System:**
High. Administrators define the rules the system must follow. Their requirements have a direct impact on the system's access control and reporting features.

**How the System Affects the Stakeholder:**
The system gives administrators one central place to manage equipment and users, replacing separate manual records.

---

## S4 — Development Team

**Role:**
The team that designs, builds, and tests the system. They are responsible for all technical decisions.

**Interest / Expectation:**
The team expects clear and stable requirements in order to work on the project in a consistent and organized manner.

**Influence on the System:**
High. The team decides which features can be implemented and which cannot, based on technical and time constraints.

**How the System Affects the Stakeholder:**
Building the system gives the team hands-on experience applying software engineering concepts in a real project. The quality of their work is directly reflected in their course assessment and grade.

---

## S5 — Faculty and Instructors

**Role:**
Indirect users of the system. While they do not borrow equipment themselves, they may need to check availability for their lab sessions.

**Interest / Expectation:**
Instructors expect to know whether certain equipment is available for their planned lab sessions.

**Influence on the System:**
Low. They may raise concerns that will be communicated through the administrators.

**How the System Affects the Stakeholder:**
The system gives faculty a way to check equipment availability without contacting lab staff directly. Equipment shortages or scheduling conflicts become more visible and easier to address in advance.
